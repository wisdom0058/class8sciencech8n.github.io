Why can't we separate hydrogen and oxygen in water by physical means?

In water, the particles of hydrogen and oxygen are so tightly attached to each other that no physical method (heating, cooling, filtering, evaporating) can pull them apart. Only a chemical change like electrolysis does it. That is why water is a compound.

Compounds are formed when different elements combine chemically in a fixed ratio to form something entirely new. Three key facts:

  • The properties of a compound are different from those of its constituent elements.
  • The elements are present in a fixed ratio. In water the ratio of the number of hydrogen atoms to oxygen atoms is always 2 : 1.
  • The constituent elements cannot be separated by any physical method.
An element's molecule has identical atoms; a compound's molecule has atoms of different elements joined chemically in a fixed ratio (water: two hydrogen, one oxygen).
How different can a compound be from its elements? Hydrogen is a fuel (it burns), oxygen supports combustion, yet water — made of nothing but these two — extinguishes fire! Because the atoms are chemically joined into a new kind of particle, the new particle has its own, new properties.

Sodium chloride (common salt)

Sodium is a soft, highly reactive metal and chlorine is a hazardous greenish gas. Combined chemically in a 1 : 1 ratio they form sodium chloride — a harmless, taste-enhancing solid essential for our lives. Dissolved salt can be recovered from water by the physical process of evaporation (that separates the mixture), but salt cannot be split into sodium and chlorine by any physical process — so salt is a compound.

Activity 8.4 — is sugar an element or a compound?

Put a teaspoon of sugar in a boiling tube and heat gently (teacher present). The sugar turns brown, then chars (blackish). Small droplets of water appear inside the tube near its open end. Charcoal (carbon) is left behind — scoop it into a watch glass and check that it burns like coal.

Activity 8.4: as sugar is heated it browns, chars and leaves black carbon; water droplets appear near the mouth of the tube. Sugar splits into simpler substances, so it must be a compound of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen.
Sugar → Carbon + Water (on heating)
Where did the water come from — the sugar or the air? We are heating the tube, so water vapour from air would not condense on its warm walls; the water must come from the sugar itself. Since sugar gives carbon and water, and water is hydrogen + oxygen, sugar contains carbon, hydrogen and oxygen. A substance that breaks into simpler substances cannot be an element — sugar is a compound of C, H and O.

❓ Frequently asked questions

What is a compound?

A pure substance formed when two or more elements combine chemically in a fixed ratio, having properties different from its elements; the elements cannot be separated by physical methods.
