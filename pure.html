What does "pure" mean?

Packs of milk, ghee and spices say "pure". In common usage, pure means unadulterated. Adulteration is the illegal adding of cheaper or poor-quality substances to a product to increase quantity or cut cost — it lowers quality and can be hazardous to health.

In science, a pure substance is one that has no other substance present in it — it consists of the same type of particles throughout and cannot be separated into other kinds of matter by any physical process. So, for a scientist, "pure" milk is actually a mixture (water, fat, proteins, sugar), while distilled water or sugar is pure.

Why can't a physical process split a pure substance? Physical processes (filtering, evaporating, using a magnet, dissolving) only sort particles that are already different. In a pure substance every particle is identical, so there is nothing to sort out.
Classify: milk → mixture; packed fruit juice → mixture; baking soda → pure substance (compound); sugar → pure substance (compound); soil → mixture.

The map of matter

Everything made of matter fits in this tree. Pure substances have only one kind of particle; mixtures contain two or more substances that keep their own properties.

Types of pure substances — is water an element?

Water can be frozen to ice and boiled to vapour and brought back — the particles of water stay the same in these physical changes. But what if we pass electricity through it?

Activity 8.3 (teacher demonstration): Fill a beaker 2/3 with water and add a few drops of dilute sulfuric acid. Fill two small test tubes completely with this water and invert them over the two terminals of a 9 V battery placed in the beaker. Gas bubbles form at both terminals. After 10–15 minutes, the volume of gas in the two tubes is not equal. Test each gas with a burning candle: one gives a 'pop' sound → hydrogen; in the other the flame glows brighter → oxygen. (Safety: teacher supervision, careful with acid, no lithium-ion battery.)
Activity 8.3: gas bubbles collect at both terminals. Twice as much hydrogen (at −) as oxygen (at +) is collected — water breaks into two different elements, so water is NOT an element.
Water → Hydrogen + Oxygen
Why can't the collected gases be water vapour? Water vapour would have condensed back to liquid water in the cool tube. These gases do not; and they behave completely differently — one burns, one supports burning. So passing electricity breaks water down into two different substances: this is a chemical change (recall Grade 7). Water is therefore not the simplest kind of substance.

Elements

Hydrogen and oxygen obtained above are pure substances called elements. Each element is made of identical particles called atoms, different from the atoms of every other element. Elements cannot be broken down further into simpler substances; they are the building blocks of all matter. Other examples: gold, silver, sulfur, carbon, iron, aluminium.

An element's molecule has identical atoms; a compound's molecule has atoms of different elements joined chemically in a fixed ratio (water: two hydrogen, one oxygen).

Atoms of most elements cannot exist independently. Two or more such atoms combine to form a stable particle called a molecule: two hydrogen atoms form one hydrogen molecule; two oxygen atoms form one oxygen molecule.

Metals, non-metals and metalloids

  • Metals: gold, silver, magnesium, iron, aluminium, copper, mercury.
  • Non-metals: carbon, sulfur, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, bromine.
  • Metalloids (in-between properties): silicon, boron.
A step further: 118 elements are known; most are solids. Eleven are gases at room temperature — all non-metals (oxygen, nitrogen, helium…). Only two are liquids: mercury (a metal) and bromine (a non-metal). Gallium and caesium are solids that melt at about 30 °C — they can melt in your palm. More than 45 elements go into a mobile phone!

Q1. In science, a pure substance…
Correct answer: B. consists of the same type of particles throughout
A pure substance has no other substance in it and is made of identical particles; it may be an element or a compound.
Q2. Which is a pure substance in the scientific sense?
Correct answer: C. Sugar
Sugar is a compound (one type of particle); milk, soil and juice are mixtures.
Q3. When electricity is passed through acidified water, the gases collected are…
Correct answer: B. hydrogen and oxygen
Water breaks down into hydrogen (pop sound) and oxygen (flame glows brighter).
Q4. In Activity 8.3 the volume of hydrogen collected is…
Correct answer: C. double that of oxygen
Water has hydrogen : oxygen atoms in 2 : 1, so twice the volume of hydrogen is collected.
Q5. A burning candle brought near a test tube gives a 'pop' sound. The gas is…
Correct answer: C. hydrogen
Hydrogen burns with a pop; oxygen makes the flame glow brighter.
Q6. Elements are…
Correct answer: C. substances that cannot be broken down into simpler substances
Elements are the simplest substances, made of identical atoms; they are the building blocks of matter.
Q7. The only two elements liquid at room temperature are…
Correct answer: C. mercury and bromine
Mercury (a metal) and bromine (a non-metal). Gallium and caesium melt around 30 °C.
Q8. Silicon and boron are examples of…
Correct answer: C. metalloids
They have properties intermediate between metals and non-metals — metalloids.

❓ Frequently asked questions

What is a pure substance in science?

Matter made of only one type of particle, which cannot be separated into other kinds of matter by any physical process. Elements and compounds are pure substances.

Why is 'pure' milk not pure for a scientist?

Milk contains water, fat, proteins and sugars — more than one substance — so scientifically it is a mixture even if unadulterated.

🎧 Listen (Hinglish explanation)
How does Activity 8.3 prove water is not an element?

Passing electricity breaks water into two different gases — hydrogen (pop) and oxygen (brighter flame) — in a 2 : 1 volume ratio. Anything that splits into simpler substances cannot be an element.

🎧 Listen (Hinglish explanation)
What is the difference between an atom and a molecule?

An atom is the identical particle of an element. Most atoms cannot exist alone, so two or more join to form a stable molecule (two H atoms → H₂ molecule).

How many elements are there and in what states?

118 known; most solids, 11 gases (all non-metals), and only two liquids at room temperature — mercury and bromine.

🎧 Listen (Hinglish explanation)
What are metalloids?

Elements like silicon and boron whose properties lie between those of metals and non-metals.
