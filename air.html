Air is a uniform mixture of gases

About 100 gas particles drawn in proportion: 78 nitrogen, 21 oxygen, 1 argon, a trace of carbon dioxide and water vapour. The grey specks are dust — suspended in air but not a component of it.

Air is a uniform mixture of mainly nitrogen (about 78%), oxygen (about 21%), argon, carbon dioxide and water vapour. Oxygen is needed by most living beings to stay alive and it supports combustion. Nitrogen does not take part in combustion. The water vapour in air turns into tiny liquid droplets when warm air touches a cool surface (that is why a cold glass "sweats").

Why is air a mixture and not a compound? Each gas in air keeps its own properties — oxygen still supports burning, nitrogen still does not — and the gases are only mixed, not chemically joined. Their proportions can also vary (e.g., water vapour changes from place to place), while a compound always has a fixed composition.

Activity 8.1 — proving carbon dioxide is present in air

  1. Half fill a glass tumbler with water and slowly add a small amount of calcium oxide (quick lime). It reacts vigorously with water, forming calcium hydroxide and releasing heat — perform with care.
  2. Stir to make a solution of calcium hydroxide, called lime water; filter it. It is colourless.
  3. Leave it in a petri dish for a few hours, stirring at intervals. It slowly turns milky.
Carbon dioxide particles from air reach the lime water and react with calcium hydroxide, making tiny insoluble white particles of calcium carbonate — the solution turns milky.
Calcium hydroxide + Carbon dioxide → Calcium carbonate + Water
Why does it turn milky? Carbon dioxide from the air reacts with the calcium hydroxide to form calcium carbonate — tiny insoluble white particles — and water. The suspended white particles scatter light, making the liquid look milky. Since it turns milky just by standing in air, carbon dioxide must be present in air.

Activity 8.2 — what else is in air? Dust

Place a clean black sheet of paper undisturbed near an open window or in a garden for a few hours. Tiny particles settle on it; look with a magnifying glass.

This shows that dust particles are suspended in air. They are not an integral part of air — they are pollutants. Their nature and number vary from time to time and place to place (that is why you see shining specks in a sunbeam in a dark room).

A step further — the major air pollutants are particulate matter (dust, soot) and gases like carbon monoxide, ozone, nitrogen dioxide and sulfur dioxide. The Air Quality Index (AQI) is the tool used to describe how clean or polluted the air is.

Q1. Air is a…
Correct answer: B. uniform mixture

Q2. The gas that makes up about 78% of air is…
Correct answer: C. nitrogen
Nitrogen ≈ 78%; it does not take part in combustion.
Q3. Lime water is a solution of…
Correct answer: C. calcium hydroxide
Calcium oxide (quick lime) + water → calcium hydroxide; its solution is lime water.
Q4. Lime water left in air turns milky because of the formation of…
Correct answer: B. calcium carbonate
CO₂ in air + calcium hydroxide → calcium carbonate (insoluble white particles) + water.
Q5. Which statement about dust in air is correct?
Correct answer: B. Dust is a pollutant suspended in air
Dust particles are suspended but not part of air's composition; they are pollutants whose amount varies.
Q6. When warm air touches a cool surface, droplets form because…
Correct answer: B. water vapour in air condenses
The water vapour present in air turns into liquid water on the cool surface.
Q7. AQI is a tool used to describe…
Correct answer: C. air quality
The Air Quality Index describes how polluted the air is.
