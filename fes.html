🧲 Mixture vs Compound: Iron and Sulfur

Activity 8.5 — Sample A and Sample B compared by appearance, magnet test and acid test

Activity 8.5 (teacher demonstration, well-ventilated area)

  1. Take 5.6 g iron filings (grey-black) and 3.2 g sulfur powder (yellow) on a watch glass. Mix thoroughly → Sample A.
  2. Heat half of Sample A in a china dish with continuous stirring until a black mass forms. Cool it, grind it in a mortar → Sample B.
  3. Compare A and B: appearance, magnet test, and gas test with dilute hydrochloric acid (waft gas gently; never smell directly; test with a burning splinter).
Activity 8.5: the mixture keeps iron's magnetism and shows both colours; after heating, the compound iron sulfide is uniform, non-magnetic and gives a different gas with acid — proof that a new substance formed.

What does it all mean?

Sample A is a mixture of the two elements iron and sulfur: its components retain their properties (iron is still magnetic, still reacts with acid to give hydrogen; sulfur is still yellow and does not react with the acid), and they can be separated with a magnet.

Sample B is a compound — iron sulfide. It has the same colour and texture throughout, it is not attracted by a magnet, it gives a different gas with acid, and iron and sulfur can no longer be separated. A completely new substance with new properties has been formed by heating.

Why does the magnet not affect Sample B? Because there are no free iron particles left. On heating, every iron particle has chemically combined with a sulfur particle to form iron sulfide particles, and iron sulfide is not magnetic. The property "magnetic" belonged to iron, not to the new compound.
Why 5.6 g and 3.2 g? These amounts contain equal numbers of iron and sulfur atoms, so they combine completely in the fixed 1 : 1 ratio of iron sulfide, leaving neither element in excess — a hint that compounds form in fixed ratios.

Classification of everything used: iron, sulfur — elements; Sample A — mixture; iron sulfide, hydrochloric acid, iron chloride, hydrogen sulfide — compounds; hydrogen — element; dilute HCl — mixture (acid + water).

Correct answer: B. burns with a pop sound

Correct answer: C. hydrogen sulfide

Correct answer: B. It is uniform, non-magnetic and gives a different gas with acid

Correct answer: C. stays as a yellow solid
Sulfur does not react with hydrochloric acid; it is left as a yellow solid.

Never smell directly; waft the fumes gently towards your nose.

❓ Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Sample A and Sample B?

Sample A is an unheated mixture of iron and sulfur — both visible, magnetic, gives hydrogen with acid. Sample B is the compound iron sulfide formed on heating — uniform black, non-magnetic, gives hydrogen sulfide with acid.

Why is Sample B not attracted by a magnet?

All the iron has chemically combined with sulfur to form iron sulfide; no free iron remains, and iron sulfide is not magnetic.

How do the gases from A and B with dilute HCl differ?

A gives hydrogen (odourless, pop sound). B gives hydrogen sulfide (rotten-egg smell). Different gases prove different substances are present.

Can iron and sulfur be recovered from Sample B?

Not by physical means. A compound's elements cannot be separated by physical methods such as a magnet.
