How do we use elements, compounds and mixtures?

  • Air we breathe — a mixture of oxygen, nitrogen, carbon dioxide…
  • Water, essential for life — a compound of hydrogen and oxygen.
  • Iron and aluminium — elements used to build bridges, buildings and vehicles.
  • Chemists study how elements combine to create compounds → medicines, vaccines, fertilisers that feed a growing population.
  • Engineers and material scientists design mixtures with special properties → the alloy stainless steel is stronger and more durable than pure iron. Wood, steel and concrete — all building materials — are mixtures.
A step further: graphene aerogel, made from carbon, is said to be the lightest material on Earth — a blade of grass can hold it. Being highly porous it absorbs a lot, so it may clean up oil spills, and is useful in energy-saving devices and building coatings.
Why is knowing "element / compound / mixture" the key to innovation? Because it tells you what can be changed and how: mixtures can be tuned by changing proportions (alloys), while compounds are made or broken only by chemical reactions (medicines, fertilisers). The classification tells a scientist which tool to use.

What are minerals?

Most rocks are mixtures of minerals. A mineral has a fixed chemical composition — usually a compound, occasionally a pure element (a native mineral like gold).

Most rocks are mixtures of minerals, visible by eye, magnifying glass or microscope. A mineral is a natural, solid substance found on Earth with a fixed chemical composition. Most minerals are compounds made of more than one element — quartz, calcite, mica, pyroxene, olivine, talc. Some, called native minerals, are pure elements: metals such as gold, silver, copper, or non-metals like sulfur, carbon.

Everyday things come from minerals: cement is made from calcite, quartz, alumina and iron oxide; talcum powder from the mineral talc.

How can gold be both a mineral and a metal? As found in nature — a natural solid with a fixed composition (pure gold) — it fits the definition of a (native) mineral. As a substance, it is an element with metallic properties (lustre, malleability, conductivity), so it is a metal. The two words describe two different things: where it comes from, and what kind of substance it is.

Our scientific heritage — Dhokra art

Dhokra is an old craft of Bihar and Odisha. A design is shaped in beeswax, covered with clay to make a mould; the wax is melted out and the hollow is filled with molten brass or bronze (alloys — mixtures of copper with zinc or tin), giving strong, shiny golden figures of animals, people and nature.

What is NOT matter?

Elements and compounds are the building blocks of matter — everything that has mass and takes up space. But light, heat, electricity, thoughts and emotions are important parts of our world that are not made of matter.

Why is stainless steel preferred over pure iron?

Mixing iron with nickel, chromium and a little carbon gives an alloy that is stronger, more durable and rust-resistant than pure iron.
